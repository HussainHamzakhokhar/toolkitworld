JPG and PNG are the two most common image formats on the web, and sooner or later you will need to convert one to the other — perhaps because a website only accepts PNG, or because you want to edit a photo without losing more quality. This guide explains the real differences between the formats, when converting is worthwhile, what it cannot do, and how to do it on any device.

## JPG and PNG in a nutshell

| | JPG (JPEG) | PNG |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | Lossy — discards some detail | Lossless — keeps every pixel |
| Best for | Photos and complex images | Screenshots, logos, text, graphics |
| Transparency | Not supported | Supported |
| Typical photo size | Small | Large (often several times bigger) |

**JPG** was designed for photographs. It throws away detail the eye is least likely to notice, which makes files much smaller. The downside is that every time a JPG is edited and saved again, a little more detail is lost, and sharp edges — such as text — can get blurry "halos".

**PNG** stores images exactly. It is ideal for graphics with sharp edges and flat colours, and it supports transparent backgrounds. For photos, however, PNG files are usually much larger.

## When converting JPG to PNG makes sense

- **A website or app requires PNG.** Some upload forms, design tools or print services only accept PNG.
- **You plan to edit the image repeatedly.** Saving as PNG between edits prevents additional JPG compression loss.
- **You want to add transparency later.** You will still need an editor to remove the background, but PNG can store the result.
- **You want an exact copy for archiving** after other processing.

## What converting cannot do

Converting a JPG to PNG **does not restore quality**. The PNG will be a perfect copy of the JPG — including any blur, blockiness or artefacts from the original compression. It also will not create a transparent background automatically: a JPG with a white background becomes a PNG with a white background.

Expect the PNG to be bigger. A 2 MB photo can easily become 6–10 MB as a PNG. If file size matters, keep the JPG or consider WebP.

## Converting with built-in apps

You can also convert with software you already have:

- **Windows:** open the image in Paint, choose *File → Save as → PNG picture*.
- **macOS:** open the image in Preview, choose *File → Export*, and select PNG as the format.
- **iPhone and Android:** gallery apps rarely offer format conversion, so a browser-based converter is usually the easiest option.

## Going the other way: PNG to JPG

Converting PNG to JPG is the common choice when you want a smaller file — for email, websites or uploads with size limits. Two things to decide:

1. **Background colour.** JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas need a colour. White suits documents; match the background of the page where the image will appear.
2. **Quality.** For photos, 80–90% usually looks identical to the original. For screenshots or text, use 90% or higher, or keep PNG.

## Should I use WebP instead?

WebP is a newer format supported by all major browsers. It produces smaller files than JPG at similar quality and supports transparency like PNG. It is a great choice for your own website. However, some older software and upload forms still do not accept it, so JPG and PNG remain the safest formats for sharing. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is PNG higher quality than JPG?

PNG is lossless, so it never loses detail when saved. But converting a JPG to PNG cannot add back detail the JPG already lost.

### Why is my PNG so much larger than the JPG?

Because PNG keeps every pixel exactly, while JPG uses lossy compression designed for photos.

### Will converting remove the background?

No. The background stays as it is. Removing a background requires an image editor or a dedicated background-removal tool.

## Conclusion

Convert JPG to PNG when you need PNG specifically — for compatibility, repeated editing or transparency. It will not improve quality and the file will usually grow. For smaller files, go the other way and convert PNG to JPG with a suitable background and quality setting.
