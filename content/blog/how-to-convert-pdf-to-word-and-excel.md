PDF is designed to look the same everywhere, which makes it perfect for sharing and terrible for editing. Sooner or later you will need to get text out of a PDF and into Word, or a table into Excel. This guide explains why PDF conversion is never perfect, which files convert well, and how to get the best results — plus the reverse: turning Word and Excel files into PDFs.

## Why converting a PDF is harder than it sounds

A Word document knows that a sentence belongs to a paragraph, that a paragraph sits under a heading, and that a number lives in a particular table cell. A PDF usually does not. Most PDFs store small pieces of text, each placed at exact coordinates on the page, together with the fonts needed to draw them.

So a converter has to *reconstruct* the structure by looking at the layout:

- Pieces of text on the same horizontal line form a **line**.
- Lines close together with the same font size form a **paragraph**.
- Noticeably larger or bolder text is probably a **heading**.
- Text arranged in aligned vertical columns is probably a **table**.

This works well most of the time, but it is educated guesswork. That is why no converter — free or paid — can guarantee an identical result for every PDF.

## Which PDFs convert well?

**Good candidates:**

- PDFs exported from Word, Google Docs or other word processors.
- Reports and statements generated by software, with clear columns.
- Simple single-column documents such as letters, CVs and articles.

**Difficult cases:**

- **Scanned documents.** A scan is a picture of a page, so it contains no text at all. It needs OCR (optical character recognition) first. A good converter will tell you when it finds no text rather than giving you an empty document.
- **Multi-column layouts** such as newsletters and academic papers, where the reading order is not obvious.
- **Complex tables** with merged cells, cells containing several lines, or tables drawn as images.
- **Designed documents** such as brochures, where text is placed freely around images.

## Converting PDF to Excel

Use PDF to Excel when you need to **work with numbers** — sort a statement, total a price list or chart results.

The [PDF to Excel converter](/pdf-tools/pdf-to-excel/) groups text into rows, finds where columns start across the whole page, and places each value in its column, even when some cells are empty. With "Convert numbers" switched on, values like `1,234.50`, `$99`, `(12.5)` and `45%` become real numbers, so formulas work straight away.

**Always check the result.** After converting a financial document, compare a few totals with the original. Wrapped text or merged cells can occasionally put a value in a neighbouring column, which is quick to fix once you spot it.

Dates are deliberately kept as text. A date like 03/04/2026 means 3 April in some countries and 4 March in others, and guessing wrong silently corrupts data. Convert them in Excel once you know the format.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my converted Word document look different from the PDF?

Because the converter rebuilds an editable structure from text positions. Fonts, spacing and layout are simplified on purpose so the document is easy to edit.

### How do I convert a scanned PDF?

You need OCR software first. Some scanners and phone scanning apps can create "searchable PDFs" that already contain a hidden text layer, and those can then be converted normally.

### Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

Only after removing the protection with the password in a PDF reader. Converters cannot bypass encryption.
