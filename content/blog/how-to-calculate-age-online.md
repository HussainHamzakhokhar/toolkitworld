"How old are you?" sounds simple, but forms, visas, insurance policies and school admissions often need an *exact* age — in years, months and days — on a specific date. This guide explains how exact age is calculated, why months make it slightly tricky, what happens with leap-year birthdays, and how to count the total days someone has been alive.

## Age in whole years

Your age in years is the number of birthdays you have had. To work it out:

1. Subtract your birth year from the current year.
2. If your birthday has not happened yet this year, subtract 1.

Someone born on 14 March 1995 is 31 on 25 November 2026: 2026 − 1995 = 31, and their 2026 birthday has already passed.

## Exact age in years, months and days

For an exact age, count forward from the date of birth in this order:

1. **Whole years** — the last birthday that has passed.
2. **Whole months** — from that birthday, count complete months.
3. **Remaining days** — count the days left over.

**Example:** born 14 March 1995, age on 25 November 2026.

- The last birthday was 14 March 2026 → **31 years**.
- From 14 March to 14 November 2026 is **8 whole months**.
- From 14 November to 25 November is **11 days**.

The exact age is **31 years, 8 months and 11 days**.

## Why months cause confusion

Months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days, so "one month later" is not always obvious. What is one month after 31 January? There is no 31 February.

The usual convention — and the one our [Age Calculator](/calculators/age-calculator/) follows — is that when the day does not exist in a month, the month ends on its last day. So one month after 31 January 2025 is 28 February 2025. Counting this way means the months and days are never negative and always add up to the real calendar.

Some methods instead subtract day numbers directly and "borrow" days from the previous month, which can give slightly different answers around month ends. Neither is wrong, but if an official form specifies a method, follow it.

## Total days lived

To count the total number of days between your birth date and today, you need to include every leap day along the way. A quick estimate is years × 365.25, but the exact answer comes from counting the calendar.

For someone born on 14 March 1995, on 25 November 2026 the exact total is **11,579 days**. That number already includes the eight leap days (29 February) between those dates.

Leap years follow a rule that catches many people out:

- A year divisible by 4 is a leap year (2024, 2028),
- **except** years divisible by 100 (1900, 2100 are not leap years),
- **unless** divisible by 400 (2000 *was* a leap year).

You can get weeks from days by dividing by 7: 11,579 days is 1,654 weeks and 1 day.

## Leap-day birthdays (29 February)

People born on 29 February only have a true birthday every four years. In other years, they usually celebrate on 28 February or 1 March. Legally, the date on which they "officially" turn a year older differs between countries — some use 28 February, others 1 March.

Our calculator treats 28 February as the birthday in non-leap years and tells you when it has done so. If a legal deadline depends on the exact date, check the rule in your country.

## Finding the day of the week you were born

Every date falls on a fixed weekday. 14 March 1995 was a **Tuesday**. Calculating this by hand uses formulas such as Zeller's congruence, but it is much easier to let a calculator look it up in the calendar.

## Calculating age on a specific date

Many applications ask for your age "on 1 September" or "at the closing date" rather than today. The method is identical — just use that date instead of today. In the [Age Calculator](/calculators/age-calculator/), change the "Age on this date" field.

This is also useful for working out how old someone was at a historical event, or will be at a future one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the time of birth matter?

For legal age, almost never — most systems count whole days. If you need age in hours or minutes, you would need the exact time and time zone of birth, which is why our calculator labels hours as approximate.

### Why does my age in days not equal my age in years × 365?

Leap years add an extra day about every four years. Over 30 years, that adds roughly 7 or 8 days.

## Conclusion

Exact age is just counting: whole years, then whole months, then the remaining days, using the real lengths of months and including leap days. The only subtle cases are month-end dates and 29 February birthdays. When precision matters, use a calculator that shows how it handled those cases.
