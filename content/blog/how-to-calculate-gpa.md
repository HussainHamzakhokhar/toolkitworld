Your grade point average (GPA) summarises your academic results as a single number. It matters for scholarships, graduate applications, internships and academic standing — so it is worth understanding exactly how it is calculated. This guide walks through the process step by step, explains why credit hours matter, and shows how to combine semesters into a cumulative GPA.

## The three ingredients

Every GPA calculation uses three things:

1. **Grades** for each course (A, B+, 85%, O, and so on).
2. **Grade points** — the number your institution assigns to each grade.
3. **Credit hours** (also called credits or units) — how much each course "weighs".

## Step 1: Convert grades to grade points

Your institution publishes a table that converts letter grades into points. A widely used 4.0 scale looks like this:

| Grade | Points | Grade | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| A / A+ | 4.0 | C+ | 2.3 |
| A− | 3.7 | C | 2.0 |
| B+ | 3.3 | C− | 1.7 |
| B | 3.0 | D | 1.0 |
| B− | 2.7 | F | 0.0 |

This is only an example. Some universities give A+ a value of 4.3, some do not use plus and minus grades at all, and many countries use completely different systems — for example a 5-point scale or a 10-point scale. **Always use your own institution's table.** The [GPA Calculator](/calculators/gpa-calculator/) includes several common scales and lets you edit any value.

## Step 2: Multiply by credit hours

For each course, multiply the grade points by the course's credit hours. The result is called **quality points**:

| Course | Credits | Grade | Points | Quality points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calculus | 4 | A− | 3.7 | 14.8 |
| English Composition | 3 | B+ | 3.3 | 9.9 |
| Chemistry | 4 | B | 3.0 | 12.0 |
| History | 3 | A | 4.0 | 12.0 |
| PE | 1 | A | 4.0 | 4.0 |

## Step 3: Add up and divide

Add the quality points and the credit hours, then divide:

- Total quality points: 14.8 + 9.9 + 12.0 + 12.0 + 4.0 = **52.7**
- Total credit hours: 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 1 = **15**
- GPA = 52.7 ÷ 15 = **3.51**

> GPA = Σ (grade points × credit hours) ÷ Σ credit hours

## Why weighting by credits matters

If you simply averaged the five grade points (3.7, 3.3, 3.0, 4.0, 4.0) you would get 3.60 — noticeably higher than the correct 3.51. That is because the 1-credit PE course would count as much as the 4-credit chemistry course. Weighting by credits makes heavier courses count proportionally more, which is why it is the standard method.

## Calculating cumulative GPA

Your cumulative GPA (often called CGPA) covers all semesters so far. You cannot average semester GPAs directly unless every semester has the same number of credits. Instead:

1. Multiply your previous cumulative GPA by the credits it covers to get previous quality points.
2. Add this semester's quality points.
3. Divide by the total credits.

**Example:** after two years you have a 3.20 GPA over 60 credits. This semester you earned 3.51 over 15 credits.

- Previous quality points: 3.20 × 60 = 192
- This semester: 52.7
- Cumulative GPA = (192 + 52.7) ÷ 75 = **3.26**

## Planning for a target GPA

You can rearrange the formula to find what you need. Suppose you want a cumulative 3.40 after 90 credits and currently have 3.26 over 75. You need 3.40 × 90 = 306 quality points in total, so you need 306 − 244.7 = 61.3 from the next 15 credits — a semester GPA of about 4.09. On a 4.0 scale that is impossible in one semester, which tells you to plan over a longer period. Trying different grades in the calculator is a quick way to explore this.

## Things that can change your official GPA

Institutions have rules that a simple calculation may not capture:

- **Pass/fail courses** usually carry credits but no grade points, so they are left out of GPA.
- **Repeated courses** may replace the earlier grade, or both attempts may count.
- **Transfer credits** often count toward graduation but not toward GPA.
- **Rounding** differs — some institutions truncate to two decimals, others round.

If your calculated GPA differs from your transcript, one of these is the likely reason.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good GPA?

It depends on the scale and the context. On a 4.0 scale, 3.0 is often treated as a solid "B" average, and 3.5 or above is strong for many competitive programmes — but requirements vary widely, so check what the specific scholarship or programme asks for.

### Can I convert my GPA to a percentage?

Only using the formula your institution or the receiving organisation specifies. There is no universal conversion, and applying a generic formula can misrepresent your results.

### Do I include failed courses?

Usually yes: an F carries credits and 0 points, which lowers your GPA. If the course is retaken, your institution's repeat policy decides what counts.

### What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA in high school?

Some high schools add extra points for honours or advanced courses (a "weighted" GPA, which can exceed 4.0). An "unweighted" GPA uses the standard scale for every course. You can model a weighted scale in our calculator by editing the grade points.

## Conclusion

Calculating GPA takes three steps: convert grades to points, multiply by credits, and divide the total points by total credits. Use your own institution's grade table, weight every course by its credits, and combine semesters using quality points rather than averaging GPAs.
